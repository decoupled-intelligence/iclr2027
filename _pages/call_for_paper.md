### Call for Papers

Negative results and arguments for tight coupling are as welcome as evidence for separation. Showing that a capability should remain end-to-end is a contribution to the workshop's central question.

**Theme 1: Architectures and Interfaces for Coupled and Decoupled Intelligence**

- Separation or integration of reasoning, knowledge, memory, agency, preferences, identity, and control
- Modular and compositional systems; memory modules and harnesses for foundation models
- Knowledge externalization and offloading
- Shared versus modality-specific representations
- Interfaces between foundation models and external components

**Theme 2: When Should Capabilities Be Coupled or Decoupled?**

- Empirical or theoretical studies of when end-to-end coupling or separation is beneficial
- Measures of coupling, modularity, specialization, and interface dependence
- Trade-offs between joint learning and explicit decomposition
- Beneficial versus harmful memorization
- Generalization across temporal, modality, task, and domain shifts
- Plasticity and continual learning
- Failure modes introduced by modular interfaces

**Theme 3: Reliability, Safety, Privacy, and User Control**

- Effects of architectural boundaries on reliability, controllability, safety, security, privacy, and auditability
- User-controlled memory and personalized AI
- Value pluralism and customizable alignment
- External verification and oversight
- Data ownership and governance
- Security and coordination failures in modular or agentic systems
- Comparisons between coupled and decoupled approaches

**Theme 4: Foundations and Limits of Decomposition**

- Practical and theoretical limits of modularization
- Irreducible interactions among reasoning, knowledge, memory, and agency
- Cognitive abstractions and shared representations across modalities
- Boundaries between models, agents, users, tools, and environments
- Philosophical and cognitive-science perspectives on the organization of intelligence

### Submission Tracks and Formatting

We invite submissions through OpenReview, reviewed double-blind using the ICLR 2027 template:

- **Full papers:** up to 8 pages.
- **Tiny papers:** up to 2 pages.

Page limits exclude references and appendices. The template and submission link will be posted when the call opens.

Tiny papers welcome modest, self-contained contributions: a simple unpublished idea and its evaluation, a small theoretical result, a follow-up experiment or re-analysis, or a fresh perspective on existing work. We particularly welcome students, independent researchers, and authors outside the ML conference circuit.

### Publication and Presentation

The workshop is **non-archival**. We will not accept papers already published at a peer-reviewed venue, including the ICLR 2027 main conference. Work under review elsewhere is welcome where the other venue's policy allows.

Every accepted paper will be presented during a poster session. Selected submissions will receive contributed oral presentations, with attention to representing both coupled and decoupled perspectives. Accepted papers will be public on OpenReview and linked from the website.

### Review Process and Conflicts of Interest

Each submission will receive at least two reviews, with an organizer acting as area chair. Decisions are made jointly by the organizers. Reviewers and area chairs will not handle papers from their own organization, recent collaborators, or other declared conflicts. Conflicted organizers will not participate in decisions.

Organizer submissions will be handled entirely by organizers from other institutions.

### LLM Usage Policy

We follow the ICLR 2027 policies on large language model usage. AI systems may not serve as primary authors or reviewers. AI-generated papers are not permitted in the tiny-paper track: AI assistance is allowed, but submissions must be primarily human-authored. We plan to use conference-provided tools to flag AI-generated papers and reviews.

### Access

Authors unable to travel due to exceptional circumstances, such as visa delays, may provide a pre-recorded presentation, and organizers may display their posters on their behalf.
